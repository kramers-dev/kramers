# K0029. Most probable rupture force under constant loading (Bell-Evans)

**Area:** Driven escape and switching under a ramp. **Kind:** exact (exact within the stated model).

A bond whose lifetime falls exponentially with force (Bell's model) breaks most often at the force (kT/x) ln(r x/(k0 kT)) when the force rises at rate r: the logarithmic loading-rate law of dynamic force spectroscopy.

## Model

$$ k(F)=k_0\,e^{Fx/k_BT},\quad F=\dot F\,t $$

## Result

$$ F^*=\frac{k_BT}{x}\ln\!\left[\frac{\dot F\,x}{k_0k_BT}\right] $$

## Use it

```python
from kramers.formulas import bell_evans_force
bell_evans_force(loading_rate: 'float', k0: 'float', x: 'float', kT: 'float' = 1.0) -> 'float'
```

```text
F* = (kT/x) ln(loading_rate x / (k0 kT)), the mode of the rupture-force density.
```

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `loading_rate` | dF/dt |
| `k0` | zero-force rate |
| `x` | distance to the transition state |
| `kT` | thermal energy |

## When it holds

Bell's rate law (force-independent x), single barrier, slow loading compared with molecular relaxation, r x > k0 kT. Exact for that model.
