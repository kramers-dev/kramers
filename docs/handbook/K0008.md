# K0008. Decay rate of the noisy fold at any barrier height (computed reference)

**Area:** Escape over a barrier. **Kind:** computed-reference (computed here to stated precision; no closed form is claimed).

The long-time escape rate of the noisy saddle-node normal form, as the smallest eigenvalue of its backward operator, computed to full double precision at any distance from the fold, from the bifurcation point (rate 0.354) to barriers where it is 1e-50 and below.

## Model

$$ dy = (y^2 - u)\,ds + \sqrt{2}\,dW,\ y(0)\ \text{in the well},\ \text{escape to } +\infty $$

## Result

$$ S(s) \sim c\,e^{-E_0(u)\,s},\quad E_0 = \min\operatorname{spec}(-\mathcal{L}_u) $$

## Use it

```python
from kramers.formulas import fold_decay_rate
fold_decay_rate(u: 'ArrayLike') -> 'np.ndarray | float'
```

```text
Long-time decay rate E0(u) of survival in the scaled noisy fold dy = (y^2 - u) ds + sqrt(2) dW.

``u >= -2`` is the scaled distance to the fold (u > 0: a well and a barrier of
4/3 u^(3/2) in units of the noise). In physical units, for dx = (r + x^2) dt +
sqrt(D) dW, the rate is E0(u) * (D/2)^(1/3) with u = -r / (D/2)^(2/3).

The value is the smallest eigenvalue of the backward operator, computed as the
smallest root of its characteristic function, whose Taylor coefficients are
integrals of positive functions; this keeps full relative accuracy for rates as small
as 1e-300. It is not the reciprocal mean passage time (K0004); the two agree only
when the barrier is high.
```

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `u` | scaled distance to the fold |

## Where it is printed

This entry is a computed reference: no closed form is claimed. Its checks below are its evidence.


## When it holds

Any u >= -2. Double precision; agreement with 65-digit values to about 1e-15.

## How it is checked

## Notes

The quasi-stationary decay rate and the reciprocal mean passage time are different observables: at the fold they differ by 43% (0.354 against 0.201).
