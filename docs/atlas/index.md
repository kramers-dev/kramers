# The validity atlas

Where an approximate result of the handbook stops being accurate, computed. Each map is the error of one or more entries against an exact computation over a grid of their parameters, stored as data in the package (`kramers.atlas`), with a claim whose numbers are generated from that data. A map says nothing outside its grid. Earlier work on the same questions is listed on each map, with whether we have read it; no map claims to be the first.

| Map | Entries | Status | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [M01. Kramers' overdamped rate on three wells](M01.md) | K0001 | draft | Kramers' overdamped rate is too high at every barrier from 2 to 80 kT on all three wells. At 10 kT it is 3.2%, 4.3% and 5.8% high (cubic well, quartic double well, cosine well). It is within 5% above 6.8, 8.9 and 11.4 kT, and within 1% above 29, 38.8 and 51.4 kT (each threshold placed between two grid barriers by interpolation). |
| [M02. Firing rate of the noisy saddle-node: weak-noise and weak-bias formulas](M02.md) | K0006, K0007 | draft | For dx/dt = beta + x^2 + sqrt(2D) xi both formulas depend only on b = beta/D^(2/3). The weak-noise rate is within 10% of the exact rate only for b <= -2.14 (a barrier of 4.2 noise units, 4|b|^(3/2)/3), within 5% for b <= -3.02 and within 1% for b <= -7.8 (barrier 29); closer to the bifurcation it is up to 22.2% too high (at b = -1). The weak-bias line is within 10% for -0.84 <= b <= 2.26, within 1% for -0.46 <= b <= 0.72, and gives a negative rate below b = -1.37. So for -2.14 < b < -0.84 neither formula is within 10% (within 5%: -3.02 < b < -0.7 is uncovered). |
| [M03. Force-dependent rate of Dudko, Hummer and Szabo](M03.md) | K0026 | draft | Where at least 3 kT of barrier is left at the applied force (the formula warns below that), the largest error on the grid in the force dependence of the rate is +8.8% for the linear-cubic surface (at 3.3 kT left, dG = 80 kT) and +15.6% for the cusp (at 3.2 kT left, dG = 80 kT). Every grid point is within 10% where more than 1 kT (linear-cubic) or 5.8 kT (cusp) of barrier is left, and within 5% above 6.1 kT and 10.4 kT. |
| [M04. Rupture force under constant loading: Dudko-Hummer-Szabo and Bell-Evans](M04.md) | K0027, K0029 | draft | On the linear-cubic surface the Bell-Evans most probable force is too low wherever s >= 0.25, by an amount that grows with the loading rate and hardly depends on the barrier: 8% to 10% at s = 0.5, and up to 27% at the fastest loading mapped. (Bell's model takes the distance to the barrier as fixed; on this surface it shrinks under force.) At the slowest loading on low barriers it is too high instead, by up to 19%. The Dudko-Hummer-Szabo mean force is within 5% of the exact mean for s >= 0.4, 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 at zero-force barriers of 10, 20, 40 and 80 kT, and its spread within 5% for s >= 0.5, 0.3, 0.15 and 0.1 (grid values). At slower loading, where the force rises by only a few kT/x during the zero-force lifetime 1/k0, both fail: at 10 kT and s = 0.1 the mean is off by -62% and the spread by +90%. Loading faster than s = 0.75 to 0.95 (depending on the barrier), where more than 0.1% of bonds would survive to the critical force, is left out: there the frozen-rate picture itself fails. |
| [M05. Rates from a switching histogram: bias, spread and the error bar](M05.md) | K0025 | draft | A one-sigma error bar should cover the true rate 68.3% of the time. The one kramers reports with each bin's rate does so roughly (60% to 76% on this grid) wherever at least 10 switches are expected both inside the bin and after it, and as rarely as 3% for bins where fewer than one switch is expected: such a bin often reports a rate of zero with an error bar of zero. The estimate itself is biased by less than 1% where at least 30 switches are expected both inside the bin and after it, and by less than 5% where at least 5 are. |
