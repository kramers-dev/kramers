# The validity atlas

Where an approximate result of the handbook stops being accurate, computed. Each map is the error of one or more entries against an exact computation over a grid of their parameters, stored as data in the package (`kramers.atlas`), with a claim whose numbers are generated from that data. A map says nothing outside its grid. Earlier work that may bear on the same questions is listed on each map, with what of it we have read (work not opened is judged from its title only); no map claims to be the first.

| Map | Entries | Status | Claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [M01. Kramers' overdamped rate on three wells](M01.md) | K0001 | draft | Kramers' overdamped rate is too high at every barrier from 2 to 80 kT on all three wells. At 10 kT it is 3.2%, 4.3% and 5.8% high (cubic well, quartic double well, cosine well). It is within 5% above 6.8, 8.9 and 11.4 kT, and within 1% above 29, 38.8 and 51.4 kT (each threshold placed between two grid barriers by interpolation). |
| [M02. Firing rate of the noisy saddle-node: weak-noise and weak-bias formulas](M02.md) | K0006, K0007 | draft | For dx/dt = beta + x^2 + sqrt(2D) xi both formulas depend only on b = beta/D^(2/3). The weak-noise rate is within 10% of the exact rate for b <= -2.14 (a barrier of 4.2 noise units, 4|b|^(3/2)/3), within 5% for b <= -3.02 and within 1% for b <= -7.8 (barrier 29). Closer to the bifurcation it is up to 22.2% too high (at b = -1), then crosses zero near b = -0.4 on its way to -100% at b = 0, so it is within 10% again, in passing, for -0.52 <= b <= -0.32. The weak-bias line is within 10% for -0.84 <= b <= 2.26, within 1% for -0.46 <= b <= 0.72, and gives a negative rate below b = -1.37. So for -2.14 < b < -0.84 neither formula is within 10% at any grid point (within 5%: -3.02 < b < -0.7). |
| [M03. Force-dependent rate of Dudko, Hummer and Szabo](M03.md) | K0026 | draft | Where at least 3 kT of barrier is left at the applied force (the formula warns below that), the largest error in the force dependence of the rate is +9% for the linear-cubic surface (at 3 kT left, dG = 80 kT) and +15.6% for the cusp (at 3.3 kT left, dG = 80 kT). For every zero-force barrier mapped, the error is within 10% while more than 1 kT (linear-cubic) or 6.3 kT (cusp) of barrier is left, and within 5% while more than 6.4 kT and 10.5 kT is left (each threshold the first crossing as the force rises, found by root finding on exact rates). |
| [M04. Rupture force under constant loading: Dudko-Hummer-Szabo and Bell-Evans](M04.md) | K0027, K0029 | draft | On the linear-cubic surface the Bell-Evans most probable force is too low wherever s >= 0.25: by 8% to 10% at s = 0.5 across the barriers mapped, and by up to 27% at the fastest loading mapped. At the slowest loading on low barriers it is too high instead, by up to 19%. At large barriers its error approaches s / [1.5 (1 - (1 - s)^(2/3))] - 1, Bell's force s dG / x divided by the force at which this surface's barrier has fallen to (1 - s) dG (leading order); for s >= 0.2 the 80 kT row follows that curve to within 0.045 percentage points and the 20 kT row to within 0.49. The Dudko-Hummer-Szabo mean force is within 5% of the exact mean once ln(r x / (k0 kT)) exceeds 3.3 to 4.2, depending on the barrier (7 to 80 kT), and its spread once it exceeds 4.3 to 5.4 (thresholds refined between grid points by root finding). At slower loading both fail: at 10 kT and s = 0.1 the mean is off by -62% and the spread by +90%. Loading faster than s = 0.75 to 0.95 (depending on the barrier), where more than 0.1% of bonds would survive to the critical force, is left out; the frozen-rate law itself is not tested by this map. |
| [M05. Rates from a switching histogram: bias, spread and the error bar](M05.md) | K0025 | draft | A one-sigma error bar should cover the true rate 68.3% of the time. The one kramers reports with each bin's rate covers it 58.6% to 76.1% of the time wherever at least 10 switches are expected both inside the bin and after it, and 62.6% to 73.2% from 30; for bins where fewer than one switch is expected the coverage falls as low as 3%, because such a bin often reports a rate of zero with an error bar of zero. The estimate itself is biased by less than 5% where at least 10 switches are expected inside and after the bin (by up to 7.8% at 5), and by less than 1% from 50. These numbers come from a dense scan (every N from 10 to 400, 31 more to 20,000, 200 values of mu), not only the plotted grid; between scan points values can lie slightly outside them. |
